Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM) Calculator

How many years of gross rent does the property cost?

The gross rent multiplier (GRM) divides a property’s price by its annual gross rent. It is the fastest of all real-estate screens: a GRM of 10 means the price equals ten years of gross rent, before any expenses.

Because GRM ignores vacancy, taxes, insurance and financing, it is only a first filter. RentScope pairs it with NOI, cap rate and DSCR so you can move from a quick screen to a real analysis.

Formula

GRM = Purchase price ÷ Annual gross rent

Use gross scheduled rent (before vacancy) for a conventional GRM. Some investors use effective gross income instead, which produces a slightly higher, more conservative number. A lower GRM means the property is cheaper relative to its rent.

How to calculate it

  1. Multiply monthly rent by 12 to get annual gross rent.
  2. Divide the purchase price by that annual rent.
  3. Compare the GRM to similar local properties — and remember it ignores expenses and financing.

FAQ

What is a good GRM?

It varies by market and property type. Lower is generally better for the buyer, but high-appreciation markets often trade at higher GRMs. Compare against local comps, not a national number.

What is the difference between GRM and cap rate?

GRM uses gross rent and ignores operating expenses; cap rate uses NOI and therefore reflects expenses. Cap rate is the more complete measure.

Does GRM include vacancy?

A conventional GRM uses gross scheduled rent and ignores vacancy. Using effective gross income gives a more conservative version.

Is GRM the same as the 1% rule?

They are related. The 1% rule compares monthly rent to price, while GRM compares annual rent to price. A 1% ratio is roughly a GRM of 8.3.
