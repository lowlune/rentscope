50% Rule Calculator

A fast way to stress-test a rental’s expenses.

The 50% rule says that operating expenses on a rental typically consume about half of gross rent, before the mortgage. It is a back-of-the-envelope check that forces you to budget for management, maintenance, taxes, insurance and vacancy instead of assuming rent is profit.

The rule is a screening heuristic, not a law. High-tax or older properties can run well above 50%; newer, low-tax properties can run below it. RentScope computes your actual operating-expense ratio so you can compare it with 50%.

Formula

50% rule: Operating expenses ≈ 50% of gross rent

Operating expenses here include management, maintenance, property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, owner-paid utilities and a vacancy allowance. The mortgage is separate. If your actual expense ratio is below 50%, the rule is conservative for your deal; if it is above, the rule would understate your costs.

How to calculate it

  1. Take annual gross rent.
  2. Estimate total operating expenses (not including the mortgage).
  3. Divide expenses by gross rent to get your operating-expense ratio.
  4. Compare it to 50%. If it is far above, look closely at taxes, insurance and maintenance.

FAQ

Is the 50% rule accurate?

It is a rough average across many properties. It is most useful as a quick filter; always replace it with actual numbers before making an offer.

What expenses does the 50% rule include?

Management, maintenance, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities and vacancy. It does not include the mortgage.

When does the 50% rule fail?

It understates costs for old or high-tax properties and overstates them for newer, low-expense properties. It also ignores the timing of capital expenditures.

What is a good operating-expense ratio?

Lower is better, but a very low ratio can mean maintenance or capital expenditures are being deferred. A ratio near or below 50% is generally healthy for a long-term rental.
