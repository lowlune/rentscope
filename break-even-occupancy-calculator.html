Break-Even Occupancy Calculator

How full does the property need to be to avoid losing money?

Break-even occupancy is the share of gross potential income a property must collect to cover operating expenses and debt service. It is a direct measure of risk: the lower the break-even, the more vacancy the deal can absorb.

It combines the two biggest risks in rental property — vacancy and leverage — into a single percentage, which is why lenders and investors watch it closely.

Formula

Break-even occupancy = (Operating expenses + Annual debt service) ÷ Gross potential income

Gross potential income is the rent the property would collect at 100% occupancy (plus other income). If break-even occupancy is 85%, the property can sit 15% vacant before it stops covering its cash costs. Reserves and capital expenditures still have to be paid out of any surplus.

How to calculate it

  1. Total operating expenses and the annual mortgage payment.
  2. Divide by gross potential income at 100% occupancy.
  3. Compare the result to realistic vacancy in your market — the lower the better.

FAQ

What is a good break-even occupancy?

Lower is safer. Many investors look for a break-even below the market’s normal vacancy plus a margin — for example, 80–85% in markets with 5–10% vacancy. Above 90–95% leaves little room for error.

How is break-even occupancy different from vacancy rate?

Vacancy rate is what actually happens; break-even occupancy is the threshold at which the property runs out of money. The gap between them is your safety margin.

Does break-even occupancy include the mortgage?

Yes — that is the point. It uses operating expenses plus debt service, so it reflects leverage.

How can I lower break-even occupancy?

Raise rent, cut operating expenses, reduce the loan (more down payment), or refinance to a lower rate.
